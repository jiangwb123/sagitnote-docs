Context

Why researchers are moving off Weava

Weava Highlighter earned its audience: one-click highlights on web pages and PDFs, folders, and citation-friendly export. But as of November 2026, its Chrome Web Store listing hasn't shipped an update since February 2024, and its recent reviews average around 2.7 stars — with the recurring themes being sync problems, broken highlights on modern sites, and unanswered support requests.

Nobody outside the Weava team knows its plans, and the extension still does basic highlighting. But research notes are a multi-year asset. Starting or moving a workflow onto a tool that hasn't been touched in close to three years is a risk many people would rather not take.

Workflow

What the move feels like

Reading

Highlight with keys 1–8 switching tools and C cycling colors — same one-step flow you're used to, plus style variety (underline, box, background, and more) when you want visual hierarchy, e.g. yellow for claims, green for evidence, red for objections. On PDFs the same tools just work.

Organizing

Pages collect automatically in the Annotation Manager: folder trees per course or project, tags for cross-cutting themes, favorites for the essentials, full-text search across everything you've marked.

Writing it up

Turn scattered highlights into a literature review in your own document — with KaTeX formulas, tables, and annotated screenshots — then export as Markdown straight into Obsidian or your PKM of choice. Export formats →

Sharing with an advisor or group

Migration

Moving your highlights over

There's no Weava importer, and honestly that's fine: highlights are cheap to redo, notes are not.

  • 1. Archive first. Export whatever Weava allows and keep the file — it's your record of past reading.
  • 2. Re-highlight as you go. Re-annotate sources only when you actually revisit them; most dormant highlights never come back.
  • 3. Keep the exit open. Sagitnote exports JSON, CSV, Markdown, and full backups — your new library stays yours.
Honest Take

When staying on Weava is fine

If your highlights still render, you rarely touch PDFs with complex layouts, you don't need Edge support, and you're comfortable with the maintenance risk — Weava's basic flow remains usable and free. Switching has a real, if small, re-learning cost.

The case for moving is time: research notes compound for years, and Sagitnote is actively developed, works across Chrome and Edge, keeps data local-first (no lock-in), and gives you room to grow into documents, math, and sharing without adopting yet another tool.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is Weava still maintained?

As of November 2026, Weava's Chrome Web Store listing shows no update since February 2024, and its recent reviews average around 2.7 stars. We can't speak to the team's intent — the extension still works for basic highlighting — but if you're starting a multi-year research workflow today, an actively maintained tool is a reasonable thing to want.

What does Sagitnote give Weava users?

Five highlight styles in a configurable 6-color palette on any web page and PDF; ten annotation tools beyond highlighting (arrows, notes, freehand, screenshots, element marks); folders, tags, favorites, and full-text search; your own documents with KaTeX math; export to Markdown (Obsidian-compatible), CSV, and JSON; and local-first storage with optional cloud sync. Free to start, Pro US$6 for 1 month or US$50 for 1 year.

Can I import my Weava highlights into Sagitnote?

There's no Weava importer. Export your highlights from Weava (it offers export) and keep that file as an archive; then re-highlight the source pages and PDFs in Sagitnote as you revisit them. Going forward, Sagitnote exports everything as JSON, CSV, Markdown, or a one-click full backup, so your new notes stay portable.

Does Sagitnote highlight PDFs as well as web pages?

Yes. Sagitnote opens PDFs — from the web or your disk — in an annotation-ready viewer with a selectable text layer, so the same highlight, arrow, and note tools work on PDFs and regular pages alike. Highlights re-appear the next time you open the file.

Does it work outside Chrome, and is it free?

Sagitnote runs on Chrome 109+ and Edge 109+ (Manifest V3). It's free after sign-up: all ten tools, cloud sync, and 100 new annotations per day. Pro (US$6 for 1 month or US$50 for 1 year, one-time purchase) raises limits to 2,000 new annotations per day, 20GB storage, and HTML export.
