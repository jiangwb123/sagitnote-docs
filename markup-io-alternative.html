Context

Why people search for a Markup.io alternative

Markup.io is a solid visual-collaboration platform. The friction is price: in early 2025 it retired its free plan and moved to paid per-seat subscriptions, and a noticeable wave of solo users, freelancers, and small teams started shopping around. As of November 2026, its public pricing lists plans from US$79 per user per month on annual billing — about US$948 per user per year, multiplied by every seat on the team.

That model makes sense for agencies with structured review pipelines. But if your workflow is mostly "let me point at this thing and tell you what to change", paying per seat for every person who might occasionally leave a comment gets expensive fast.

Pricing

What the price difference actually is

To be precise about what's being compared: Markup.io charges per team member — US$79 per user per month on annual billing is about US$948 per user per year (November 2026). Sagitnote Pro is a personal plan: US$6 for one month or US$50 for one year for your account, and everyone you share with reads and replies for free. A solo reviewer pays US$50 versus US$948 — but Markup.io's price buys team infrastructure Sagitnote deliberately doesn't have.

Workflow

How the daily workflow compares

Reviewing a live page

With Markup.io you typically capture the page into its workspace and annotate there. With Sagitnote, the page is the canvas: open it, toggle the extension, and draw arrows, flag elements, box regions, or leave notes directly on the live site — including inside iframes. Annotations re-anchor themselves through content changes thanks to triple anchoring (element path + content fingerprint + position ratio).

Reviewing documents

Import PDF, Word (.docx), Excel, Markdown, or HTML into a Sagitnote document, or write one from scratch. Documents behave like any page: highlight, draw, comment. Then share a link — your client or colleague opens it in any browser, no extension, no account, and can even reply or annotate if you allow it. How sharing works →

Handing work to an AI coding assistant

One thing Markup.io doesn't do: turn your visual marks into a structured, machine-readable modification prompt. Sagitnote's Generate Modification Prompts exports every annotation on a page as Markdown — XPath anchor, element tag and text, your comment — ready to paste into Claude Code, Cursor, or Copilot. Migration

What to expect when you switch

There is no direct Markup.io importer, so treat the switch as a fresh start on current projects rather than a data migration:

  • 1. Bring the artifacts, not the comments. Re-import the source PDFs, Word files, and Markdown into Sagitnote documents; annotate live pages in place.
  • 2. Re-share the links. Old Markup.io links stay where they are; new review rounds run on Sagitnote share links with password, expiry, and permissions.
  • 3. Keep your exit open. Everything in Sagitnote exports as JSON, CSV, Markdown, or a one-click full backup — no lock-in going forward.
Honest Take

When Markup.io is the better choice

Don't switch if you depend on what a per-seat platform is for: managed team workspaces with roles and guests, structured approval pipelines, integrations with tools like Jira or Slack, video annotation, and enterprise admin. If your review process is the product your agency sells, that infrastructure is worth paying for.

Sagitnote is built for the other case: individuals and small teams whose feedback loop is a person, a page, and a link — and who'd rather not rent that per seat, per year.

FAQ

Questions, answered

How is Sagitnote different from Markup.io?

Markup.io is a team feedback platform sold per seat; Sagitnote is a browser extension for Chrome and Edge with a free tier and a personal Pro plan (US$6 for 1 month or US$50 for 1 year). Sagitnote covers the daily core — pointing at live pages, annotating PDFs and documents, and collecting replies via share links — but it does not replicate Markup.io's team workspaces, roles, or enterprise admin. The two products are not feature-identical.

Is Sagitnote free?

Yes. After a free sign-up you get every annotation tool, cloud sync, and sharing (100 new annotations and 3 documents per day, 100MB cloud storage, 3 active shares). Pro raises the limits (1,000,000 annotations, 20GB storage, 200 active shares) and costs US$6 for 1 month or US$50 for 1 year — a one-time purchase for your account, not a recurring per-seat subscription.

What does Sagitnote cost per seat?

There are no seats. One Sagitnote account is US$0 on the Free plan, or US$6 for 1 month / US$50 for 1 year on Pro. Everyone you share a document with reads and replies for free in their browser — they never need an account or the extension.

Can I import my Markup.io annotations into Sagitnote?

Not directly — there is no Markup.io importer. In practice, teams bring the source material instead: PDFs, Word and Markdown files import into Sagitnote documents, and live pages can simply be annotated again in place. Sagitnote's own data exports as JSON, CSV, Markdown, or a full backup, so you are never locked in going forward.

Do reviewers need to install anything to see my feedback?

No. Share links for your documents and document snapshots open in any browser on desktop or phone. You control permissions (view, annotate, reply, edit), an optional password, and a 1–30 day expiry; replies land right on the document.
